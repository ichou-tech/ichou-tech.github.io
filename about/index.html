  • More about me…

    I came to HCI through my interest in the relationship between humans and machines. Inspired by Gilbert Simondon’s thinking on technical objects, I believe that the way we interact with them deeply shapes how we think and act. As a millennial, I grew up alongside technical objects, and growing up I became more and more aware of how strongly technology and the devices around us are changing the way we live and work. Today, with the rise of AI, we deal with technology more often than ever. Technical barriers are breaking down, and more and more people can build their own products, even without being professionals. To me, this is a moving era in which creativity is flourishing as never before, and at the same time the era in which creativity is most likely to become fixed and constrained.

    That is why I believe the way humans and technology live together will become an ever more important question. My conviction is to take seriously the interaction details we take for granted, and to keep reflecting on the place of new technologies and on how people can use them. I work on playful forms of interaction and reflective design, and I keep drawing unexpected insights and new perspectives from art practice and games.

    Honestly, my interests are really wide: I'm curious about everything. The projects I'm currently building and studying range across images, narrative, multi-agent systems, games and multimodal recognition. I'm also interested in media critique, the ecological philosophy of the Anthropocene and new materialism. Besides being an HCI researcher, I'm also an artist, working on narrative illustration, mineral pigment painting and zines.
