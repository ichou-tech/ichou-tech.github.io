# Entering the 3D Printer: Negotiations of Imprecision in Making

## In one line
The authors rework a 3D printer so that one axis can be driven by hand, and treat the resulting (im)precision as a negotiation between the designer, the code, the machine and the material.

## What they did
- Built a tangible manual-control mode that lets the designer ignore the G-code toolpath and extrude by hand, plus tools that record the process, so that each experiment becomes input for the next design. Chance and emergence are welcome.
- Annotated the printed pieces from the point of view of each participant (designer, code, machine, material) to trace where imprecision comes from.
- No interview study: the pictorial itself carries the argument.

## How the related work is built
It lines up a spectrum of how far people step into the fabrication process: interactive fabrication (Willis et al.), CAM as a creative tool for printed clay (Çapunaman), two-way sync between object and model in ReForm (Weichel et al.). Then it names their shared gap: they need bespoke systems. With Toka et al., the paper argues for staying compatible with existing ecosystems, so it changes a single axis and keeps the rest of the workflow. Somanath et al. and *Being the Machine* (Devendorf & Ryokai) open another thread: who holds the intention? A clear model of *list, shared gap, my place*.

## What stays with me
- It speaks to questions I care about: imprecision, and the machine as a participant rather than a hidden tool.
- It feels like doing ceramics together with a machine and its code, where each material brings its own constraints.
- In the end it explores the tension between analog and digital, which may be the core question of hybrid fabrication.

## Open questions
- So much reflective work on making with machines is about 3D printing. Why is there so little on image making? Could images be made in an embodied way too? Is it because deviations are more visible in 3D, or because 2D is seen as less central?
