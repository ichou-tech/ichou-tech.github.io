# How are Vibrotactile Experiences Visually Represented? A Taxonomy of Illustration Characteristics
*Wittchen, Fruchard et al., CHI 2026.* [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3772318.3790598)

## In one line
A literature review of how scientific papers illustrate vibrotactile experiences (VTX), condensed into a taxonomy of *what* is represented and *how*.

> "We focused on VTX that we define as intentionally designed experiences aimed at stimulating the user's sense of touch through vibrations elicited by mechanical vibration motors."

Research question: *What are the characteristics of VTX illustrations in scientific papers?*

## What they did
They build on Antoine et al.'s taxonomy of interaction illustrations, which already unifies taxonomies from comics and industrial design into *what* codes (scenario, users, interactive system) and *how* codes (composition, visual technique, visual characteristics), and extend it to fit VTX. A formative study then checks whether the taxonomy works in practice.

## What they found
The taxonomy helps identify what an illustration shows and how, for example which visual devices a still image uses to show an action unfolding in time. It also works as a checklist: the dimensions left unchecked point to what a drawing fails to convey.

## What stays with me
A clear model for deriving a new taxonomy from existing ones, and an impressive amount of work behind it.

## Term
*Low- or high-fidelity prototypes supporting rapid design iterations*: a design vocabulary worth keeping in mind.
