# Wall, An Eccentric Design Probe: Exploring and Exposing the Sense-and-Extract Paradigm

> "Wall is an unusual everyday smart device that invites reflection from users, designers, and researchers on the experiential, social, and ethical aspects of surveillant spatial sensing systems."

## What they did
A research through design (RtD) process of conceptualising, designing, building, using and reflecting on a functional probe that listens in real time and displays what it hears as text. The evaluation is first-person use rather than field deployment.

> "Our approach is further inspired by various experimental and speculative approaches to RtD including ludic design, counterfunctional design, slow design, reflective design, phatic design, speculative enactments, and material speculation."

Among many concepts, they explain why *Wall* was the one to build: **engaging** (entertaining and thought-provoking in homes, workplaces and public spaces), **simple** (buildable from off-the-shelf parts such as Zoom, a smartphone and a USB microphone), **social** (more than a personal device), and **aural** (microphone and text, no images or video, in a world saturated with them).

## What they found
1. **Sense-and-extract and zone interfaces**: a clear vocabulary that also applies to face recognition, spatial computing or AI companion devices, anything that senses all day without buttons.
2. **Aesthetic surveillance**: danger and pleasure, exposure and surprise are intertwined in everyday use, beyond a simple "surveillance is bad, privacy is safe" opposition.
3. **The value of errors and imperfection**: a transcription that is half wrong reads like dream-talk, which gives everyone plausible deniability and, paradoxically, more trust.
4. **Details make or break acceptability**: covering up third-party systems, disarming with DIY charm, and relying on a trusted champion user. A method for designers, and a critique of how friendly industrial design wraps data extraction.

## What stays with me
- The last paragraph of the introduction states the contributions with no wasted words.
- A classic RtD structure: divergent exploration, then a clear path into one concrete probe.
- Explaining *why this probe* through a short list of design qualities is something I want to learn: it tells the reader exactly why the prototype exists.
